# SWOT hypotheses and candidate synthesis

[17_swot.csv](17_swot.csv) contains 24 analytical statements. Every row identifies supporting evidence and a metric or test. SWOT categories are judgements about the network, not statements quoted from management. Strengths/weaknesses are primarily internal capabilities/exposures; opportunities/threats concern potential developments and external conditions.

## Candidate synthesis for later discussion

1. **Different parts of Hindalco need different supply-chain policies.** Indian upstream aluminium favours scale and reliable inputs; specialised downstream products add engineering, quality and delivery requirements. Novelis needs regional rolling/recycling and customer-loop coordination; copper needs procurement and joint-product economics. This is the strategic-fit interpretation in E063, not a claim that a single product label determines the correct policy.

2. **Integration creates opportunities for coordination while concentrating some risks.** Internal material links and co-location can reduce interfaces. They can also expose several stages to one utility, process or gateway. Evaluate total cost and customer service, rather than assuming that ownership alone guarantees efficiency [E064].

3. **Capacity expansion must be matched to feedstock, qualified demand and ramp-up.** In copper, more smelting capacity can increase the importance of concentrate access and terms [E065]. In rolling/fabrication, customer qualification and conversion yield determine when nameplate becomes saleable supply. These are analytical conditions, not evidence that approved projects will fail.

4. **Circularity is a supply-chain design problem as well as a material property.** Return collection, alloy separation, recovery, transport and customer agreements determine whether recycling provides reliable and economical inputs. A recycling announcement does not establish actual returned or recovered tonnage.

5. **Information systems require aligned decisions and incentives.** The company reports several coordination mechanisms. Establishing whether they improved outcomes needs baseline service/cost data and a clear attribution method. This collection supports discussion of mechanisms, not a quantified causal verdict.

## How to prioritise the SWOT

Score each item for impact, likelihood/time horizon, evidence strength and management controllability. Avoid treating all 24 rows as equally important or converting this checklist into unsupported numerical probabilities. The strongest later recommendations will identify a specific bottleneck or uncertainty and connect an action to a measurable result.

Possible evidence-based investigation priorities are concentrate contract coverage, qualified alternate capacity, stage-specific mass balances, inventory/lead-time distributions, and product-family service requirements. They would turn the public-source hypotheses into testable project conclusions.

**No final recommendation or investment ranking has been asserted.** The group's discussion, instructor feedback and any authorised operational evidence should determine the eventual conclusions.
