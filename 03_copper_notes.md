# Indian copper: configuration, sourcing and economics

## Supply-chain configuration

The disclosed Indian copper model centres on purchased feedstock and an integrated Dahej processing/gateway complex, with downstream rod and tube activities [E019–E027; S17–S20; S25; S38]. It should not be described as a fully captive mine-to-metal system. The country register documents sourcing exposure without inventing named overseas mines, volumes or exclusive routes.

The core process relationship is **concentrate → smelting/anodes → electrorefining/cathodes → rods or tubes**, with cathodes also saleable. The historical process source identifies precious-metal and acid streams [E023; S58], while current results corroborate the economic relevance of acid [E033; S05]. Bought cathodes can also enter downstream supply; do not assume every sold copper tonne was smelted from Hindalco-purchased concentrate.

## Concentrate procurement: academic interpretation

Concentrate procurement is not just “buy copper at the lowest quotation.” Relevant dimensions are contained copper, payable metal, impurities, recoveries, blending compatibility, freight, assay/settlement rules, working capital and supply continuity. These are general custom-smelting considerations; actual Hindalco terms require contracts or interviews.

**TC/RC** means treatment and refining charges: remuneration associated with converting concentrate into refined metal. Mine supply and smelting capacity can therefore affect conversion economics differently from the copper metal price. A high copper price or higher sales revenue does not by itself show better smelter profitability. Market pressure and relationship-based continuity are both reported [E031–E033; S43; S46; S05]; they are not contradictory.

The FY25/FY26 comparison is a useful teaching example: copper metal sales declined slightly, nominal revenue rose, and EBITDA fell. The exact inputs and percentage calculations are M17–M24 and M46–M49. Those relationships illustrate why **volume, price, product mix and conversion margin** should be analysed separately. They do not identify the full causal contribution of each driver.

Contract portfolio questions include duration, volume flexibility, take-or-pay obligations, indexation, shipment windows, quality penalties and alternative-source qualification. A long-term contract may reduce availability uncertainty while retaining price exposure. A hedge may reduce financial exposure while leaving physical shortages, quality failures and port delays unchanged.

## Domestic versus overseas supply

The historical HCL agreement is evidence of a domestic supplier relationship [E022; S20]. Its “60%” refers to HCL output, not Hindalco requirements. No defensible current Hindalco import percentage was found. Do not use an India-industry import ratio or the group's international procurement-value share as a proxy.

The due-diligence country list covers a broader raw-material scope than a mine list. Transport/transit jurisdictions can reflect logistics relationships and should not automatically become ore-origin nodes. Supplier diversity by country also does not prove resilience if suppliers share one port, shipping corridor or processing bottleneck.

Exploratory Peru discussions [E058; S47] are a potential backward-integration option, not evidence of a completed acquisition. The proposed HCL/Chile route remains a research lead [S56]; it is excluded from the operating-flow dataset.

## Joint products and reverse flows

Copper economics should include by-product revenue and handling constraints. Precious metals and sulphuric acid can support overall contribution but depend on input chemistry, recovery, demand and storage/dispatch capability. An inability to evacuate a co-product can constrain the main process; test this as a scenario rather than asserting it has occurred.

The old process map includes phosphoric acid/fertiliser activities. Their current operating status was not verified, so they remain historical context in these notes rather than operating edges in the flow table. Scrap/e-waste recycling introduces collection, disassembly/sorting, assay, blending, recovery and residue handling. Recycling capacity expressed as input waste tonnes is not equal to recovered copper tonnes. Compare routes on contained-metal yield and full recovery cost, not nominal plant size alone.

## Product configuration and strategic fit

Cathodes emphasise chemical consistency and supply reliability; rods add diameter, conductivity and downstream drawing requirements; tubes add geometry and heat-transfer/customer requirements [S16; S24; S25]. These statements describe product-related service considerations, not verified customer service-level targets.

Candidate planning segmentation is efficient bulk replenishment for common grades and more responsive allocation/finishing for specialised orders. Test it using demand variability, qualification lead time, changeovers and stockout consequences. No actual Hindalco push/pull boundary, order policy or exact safety stock has been established.

## Coordination and assurance

Procurement, operations, logistics, treasury and risk management must share a feasible feedstock plan. S19 provides specific governance evidence; S21 supplies policy requirements. The source register distinguishes a reported supplier finding, country-screening flags and general policy obligations. Neither country exposure nor a certificate supports broad claims about every supplier's conduct.

Highest-priority missing data: concentrate/cathode/scrap quantities, contained-metal basis, qualified suppliers, contract cover, blend constraints, jetty/warehouse dwell, refining yield, rod allocation, actual TC/RC contribution, co-product evacuation constraints and inventory cover.
