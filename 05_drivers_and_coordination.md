# Six drivers, coordination and obstacles

The distinctions below follow the Chopra framework [S01]. Evidence keys identify observed company mechanisms. Trade-offs, diagnostic questions and proposed measures are original SCM analysis; they do not imply Hindalco currently uses every suggested KPI.

## Logistical drivers

| Driver | Evidence anchor | Efficiency–responsiveness trade-off | Proposed measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facilities | Integrated India stages; Dahej gateway/processing; regional Novelis plants [E002; E019; E041] | Scale and specialisation versus proximity, redundancy, qualification and flexible capacity | Effective bottleneck capacity; changeover loss; yield; customer-qualified alternate capacity |
| Inventory | Company discusses buffers; disclosure does not establish exact policies [E040; E066] | Holding/financing and obsolescence cost versus availability and interruption protection | Grade-specific turns; stock cover; cycle service level; fill rate; shortage and ageing cost |
| Transportation | Dedicated mineral lane, interplant EV case, ingot rail case and logistics execution tools [E005; E035; E038; E039] | Large economical loads versus frequency, speed and routing flexibility | Freight per tonne-km; landed cost; transit variability; damage; dwell; delivery reliability |

Inventory types to distinguish are raw materials, work in process, finished goods and MRO spares. Cycle, safety, seasonal and pipeline inventory describe **reasons for holding inventory**, not separate accounting material categories. Furnace/refractory spares may have low demand but high downtime consequences; a simple annual-spend ABC classification should therefore be supplemented by criticality. No specific Hindalco spare-parts policy was verified.

In transportation analysis compare direct shipment, consolidation and warehouse-mediated delivery where actual route data support them. Do not label a warehouse a distribution centre, cross-dock or vendor-managed inventory location without evidence of its function.

## Cross-functional drivers

| Driver | Evidence anchor | Decision and trade-off | Proposed measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| Information | S&OP, ERP/transport/warehouse visibility and import-trade integration [E034–E036; E056] | Timely shared data versus integration cost, quality and unclear ownership | Forecast bias/error; inventory accuracy; event latency; schedule adherence |
| Sourcing | Captive aluminium inputs, external copper feed, external Novelis metal, supplier governance [E005; E021; E028–E031; E041] | Ownership/long-term relationships versus flexibility, concentration and total cost | Qualified supplier coverage; supply continuity; total landed cost; quality; contract exposure |
| Pricing | Metal-price lag; spot alumina tenders; metal reference market [E046; E051; E060] | Margin and risk allocation versus stable demand signals and relationship continuity | Product/customer contribution; netback; price-period mismatch; spot/contract mix |

Procurement reverse auctions and alumina sales auctions are opposite directions of trade. Supplier bill discounting concerns funds flow; it is not a sourcing-volume indicator. Transportation planning software can improve visibility, but the evidence does not establish its causal effect on cost or service without a measured baseline.

## Obstacles: five-category diagnostic

The categories below are analytical tests. They are **not verified allegations of Hindalco internal failures**.

| Chopra category | Hindalco-relevant hypothesis | Evidence/data needed | Possible response to evaluate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incentive obstacles | Purchasing rewards low price while operations bears quality/freight/inventory cost; plants maximise local utilisation | Scorecards, transfer pricing, order-profit and total-cost data | Shared end-to-end service and contribution measures; explicit cost ownership |
| Information-processing obstacles | Forecasts built from replenishment orders can confuse batching with end-use demand | Matched end-customer demand, orders, forecast revisions and stock data | Share demand and inventory context; distinguish orders from consumption |
| Operational obstacles | Long lead times, minimum vessel/rail lots, campaign sequencing and qualification can magnify order changes | Lead-time distributions, batch constraints, shortage allocation and changeovers | Feasible lot/review policy, flexible transport and qualified alternatives |
| Pricing obstacles | Temporary discounts or expected metal-price changes may shift order timing | Price calendars, customer orders and hedge/contract timing | Test stable terms and transparent indexation; avoid rewarding forward buying |
| Behavioural obstacles | Functions distrust forecasts or optimise their own stage after disruption | Interviews, exception logs and plan-overwrite history | Joint root-cause reviews, explicit assumptions and agreed escalation |

Observed concentration, disruption, price timing and input-market risk make these questions relevant, but do not prove bullwhip. The requisite matched time series were not available. An interruption caused by a fire or a tariff is an external shock; it becomes a coordination question when examining how information and decisions propagate through the network.

## A useful S&OP model for later verification

Start with product-family demand and customer commitments. Translate them into grade-specific intermediate requirements using valid yields. Constrain the plan by qualified capacity, feedstock, utilities, maintenance, transport and co-product evacuation. Compare alternatives on service, total cost, working capital and contribution. Escalate unresolved trade-offs with clear ownership; then translate the agreed plan into dispatch and purchasing decisions.

This is a proposed analytical model. Check the real sequence with the group/instructor or authorised company contacts before writing that Hindalco follows these exact steps. The available disclosure establishes the S&OP connection, not this entire workflow.
