# Novelis within Hindalco's aluminium supply chain

## Why separate it analytically

Novelis is a Hindalco subsidiary with a geographically distributed rolling/recycling network and external metal procurement [E041–E046; S29–S32]. Do not depict it simply as the overseas destination of Indian primary aluminium. Any specific India-to-Novelis shipment needs evidence of its own.

The facility CSV includes selected nodes relevant to sourced flows and recent disruptions, not all global plants. The filing distinguishes regional operations and joint-venture interests; a company-wide count is not a list of wholly owned interchangeable factories. The FY26 rolled-shipment measure differs from total shipments in the 10-K. Capacity, regional movements and shipments need matching definitions before aggregation.

## Forward and reverse configuration

General network logic: purchased primary metal, purchased sheet ingot and collected/returned scrap feed casting/rolling/finishing; qualified sheet goes to beverage packaging, automotive and other industrial customers. Production/customer scrap can return for remelting. Post-consumer collection adds different availability, sorting and contamination uncertainties. The research does not claim that all scrap follows one owned collection channel.

The JLR and Ball cases are concrete evidence of customer-linked reverse flows [E043–E044]. Their academic significance is **closed-loop coordination**: customer demand planning, return-material planning and alloy specifications interact. A reliable return stream may improve supply availability, but recovery yield, sorting cost and transport still matter. “Recyclable” is a material property; actual return rate and recycled content are different measured outcomes.

## Customer and supplier relationships

Ball's reported net-sales share and Rio Tinto's metal-purchase share are value-based concentration measures [E042; S29]. They should not be interpreted as physical flow shares or transferred to Indian operations. Long-term customer arrangements such as Ball and Ardagh [S31–S32] can support demand visibility and capacity commitments; undisclosed prices, volumes and cancellation terms remain unknown.

Automotive supply is especially useful for discussing qualification constraints: technically available rolling capacity may not immediately replace a customer's approved product route. This is an analytical explanation of substitution limits, not a claim that every alternate route failed qualification during a particular event.

## Pricing, inventory and funds flow

The filing discusses metal-price lag [E046]. Purchasing metal, holding intermediate inventory and recognising finished-product prices at different times can affect reported margins and working capital. Hedge effectiveness depends on the exposure covered; regional premiums, product timing, volume mismatch and physical availability may remain relevant. Avoid assuming that index-based selling prices eliminate all metal risk.

Inventory policy should distinguish primary metal, alloy-specific scrap, sheet ingot, WIP and finished sheet. A tonne of one grade is not automatically serviceable stock for another customer's order. Useful proposed KPIs include qualified available-to-promise tonnes, alloy-specific inventory days, scrap recovery yield and customer OTIF, alongside financial working capital.

## Recent network lessons

The Oswego interruption, Kingston tariff-related adjustment and Bay Minette ramp-up are documented in I02–I04 of the issue register. They illustrate three different capacity concepts: **unavailable existing capacity, economically reallocated capacity, and not-yet-commercial new capacity**. Combining all three into nominal nameplate obscures what can actually serve customers.

Network flexibility can absorb disruption through alternate plants and external sourcing [E048], but it has a cost and qualification limit. A useful case analysis compares time to recover a critical process with time to survive using stock, alternative qualified supply and customer schedule flexibility. No numerical recovery-buffer model can be completed from the disclosed figures alone.

For a future report, retain both US-dollar Novelis financial measures and rupee group measures in their original units unless a dated exchange-rate methodology is explicitly supplied. Novelis leverage is not consolidated Hindalco leverage; quarterly results including insurance recoveries should not be treated as an unadjusted indicator of normal operating resilience.
