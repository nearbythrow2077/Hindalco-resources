## Slide 31 · Appendix A · Reorder point and lead-time demand

*Q&A · Q&A only*

With independent daily demand, a five-day lead time multiplies the variance by five and the standard deviation by the square root of five. That gives 44.72 tonnes. A one-sided 95 percent cycle service target gives 73.57 tonnes of safety stock. Add that to 500 tonnes of mean lead-time demand to obtain a reorder point of 573.57 tonnes. A variable lead time would need an additional variance term or a simulation using actual paired histories.

## Slide 32 · Appendix B · Expected shortage, fill rate and average inventory

*Q&A · Q&A only*

At the 95 percent cycle service target, the normal loss function gives an expected shortage of approximately 0.93 tonnes per replenishment cycle. With a 604-tonne lot and backorders, the estimated product fill rate is one minus 0.93 divided by 604, or about 99.85 percent. Average on-hand inventory is approximately half the lot plus safety stock, about 376 tonnes. A stockout in five percent of cycles is compatible with this unit fill rate because each shortage is small relative to the replenishment lot.

## Slide 33 · Appendix C · Copper capacity and throughput measures

*Q&A · Q&A only*

Cathode capacity and continuous cast rod capacity describe successive processes. A cathode can be sold or converted into rod, so adding their annual capacities would double-count some material. Likewise, FY26 metal sales of 487 thousand tonnes already included 373 thousand tonnes of rod sales. Rod represented about 76.6 percent of metal sales. Before estimating utilisation we would reconcile production, opening and closing stock, external purchases, and the period and grade covered by the capacity denominator.
