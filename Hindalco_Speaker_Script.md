# Hindalco Industries — speaker script

SCM G511 · Fundamental Concepts of Supply Chain

The main talk runs to **31 minutes 30 seconds** at the suggested rehearsal pace, including time to trace the diagrams. Thanvir presents slides 1–10, Lavanya slides 11–20, and Dhruv slides 21–30. Each block has 10 minutes 30 seconds. Slides 31–35 are for questions.

## Slide 01 · Hindalco Industries: supply chain configuration and strategic fit

*Thanvir Diouf Shafeeq · 0:00–0:56*

Good morning, Dr. Routroy and everyone. I’m Thanvir Diouf Shafeeq, and I’m presenting with Lavanya Joshi and Dhruv Agrawal. Imagine a customer asking Hindalco for a tonne of aluminium next week. Before anyone can promise delivery, they need to know which alloy, in what form, from which approved line, and by what date. That question connects our three parts. I’ll trace the materials and products. Lavanya will show how the supply chain drivers shape inventory and service. Dhruv will use copper sourcing, aluminium smelting and battery foil to test where those policies fit.

## Slide 02 · Strategic fit across three metals businesses

*Thanvir Diouf Shafeeq · 0:56–1:58*

Hindalco is the Aditya Birla Group’s metals company. We have drawn three boundaries around its operations because the first planning question changes at each one. In Indian aluminium, captive ore supplies refineries and smelters. The planner asks how much common metal to produce before the customer chooses a specification. In Indian copper, the planner must secure a compatible feed blend and get it to a coastal processing plant. Novelis runs a regional rolling and recycling network. There, an order depends on an approved sheet specification and the metal available to that mill. These boundaries also tell us which quantities we can compare.

## Slide 03 · Facilities and capacity at three production stages

*Thanvir Diouf Shafeeq · 1:58–3:00*

These figures are annual capacities reported for financial year 2026. India’s primary aluminium capacity was 1.34 million tonnes per year. India’s copper cathode capacity was 0.42 million, and Novelis’s flat-rolled product capacity was 4.3 million. Imagine trying to turn that list into one shipment commitment. The first number describes primary metal, the second describes refined copper, and the third describes a later conversion stage in aluminium. We also lack actual utilisation, product mix and opening stock. For a customer order, we would select the relevant process, determine how many good tonnes it can release during the delivery window, and check the next stage.

## Slide 04 · Aluminium supply chain configuration from bauxite to finished products

*Thanvir Diouf Shafeeq · 3:00–4:04*

Start at the mine and follow the bauxite into an alumina refinery. At that point the network branches. Some output serves specialty alumina or hydrate customers and leaves the chain here. Along the main metal route, alumina goes to a smelter. Electricity and carbon support conversion to primary aluminium, which is cast into usable forms. Customers can buy a primary product, or Hindalco can roll, extrude or fabricate it further. That fork matters to aggregate planning. Before converting customer orders into upstream tonnes, we have to establish the route, material specification and yield. Otherwise, a forecast for the same final product could be counted twice.

## Slide 05 · Transportation strategy on the Baphlimali–Utkal route

*Thanvir Diouf Shafeeq · 4:04–5:05*

The clearest physical link in our research runs from the Baphlimali mine to Utkal Alumina through an 18.5-kilometre covered conveyor. A dedicated lane suits a recurring movement of bulk material. It also raises a practical question. If arrivals stop for a day, how much usable bauxite does the refinery have on hand? The other examples show connections between stages. Mahan receives alumina from Utkal and Muri, while Hirakud supplies metal to downstream sites such as Belur and Alupuram. The sources identify these connections. To assess redundancy, we would still need grade compatibility, daily volumes, transport capacity and time to switch.

## Slide 06 · Product development strategy for ingots, extrusions and enclosures

*Thanvir Diouf Shafeeq · 5:05–6:08*

Picture three customers ordering equal weights of aluminium. An ingot buyer can specify an agreed grade and dispatch lot. An extrusion buyer also needs a cross-section, a die, a tolerance and possibly a finish. A vehicle manufacturer buying a battery enclosure needs an approved design at an assembly date. Hindalco reported delivery of ten thousand enclosures to Mahindra from Chakan by April 2025. The units here are enclosures, and the figure is a cumulative delivery milestone. A design change can affect tooling, inspection and existing inventory together. For that reason, the sales promise needs information from engineering as well as from the metal store.

## Slide 07 · Copper supply chain configuration at Dahej

*Thanvir Diouf Shafeeq · 6:08–7:13*

At Dahej, follow the concentrate route through the gateway to smelting and refining. The smelter makes anodes, and the refinery produces saleable cathodes. A cathode can go to a customer or become feed for downstream products such as rods or tubes. Purchased cathodes provide another possible input to conversion. Now consider the side stream. Copper processing also creates sulphuric acid and supports precious-metal recovery. If the plant increases its main throughput, it must be able to handle and dispatch these outputs. This is why the planning boundary includes the jetty, feed quality, production stages and co-product outlets. A furnace rate alone answers only one part of the question.

## Slide 08 · Supplier uncertainty in copper sourcing

*Thanvir Diouf Shafeeq · 7:13–8:17*

Hindalco’s copper due-diligence report lists thirteen raw-material sourcing countries for August 2024 to September 2025. It identifies ten further jurisdictions in connection with transport or transit. A flagged transport jurisdiction might reflect a logistics relationship, so treating all twenty-three places as mine origins would overstate diversification. There is a second limitation. A country count gives equal weight to a small and a large source. For a planner, the test is how much compatible material could arrive in time if the largest source were interrupted. Two otherwise independent suppliers could also rely on the same port or blending stock. That shared dependency belongs in the risk assessment.

## Slide 09 · Reverse logistics in the Novelis aluminium supply chain

*Thanvir Diouf Shafeeq · 8:17–9:25*

Novelis provides a useful example of reverse logistics because the customer participates in both directions of the flow. The mill converts purchased metal and suitable scrap into qualified sheet. The customer forms the product, and clean manufacturing offcuts can return for remelting. The JLR case concerns automotive sheet and stamping scrap. The Ball agreement provides a corresponding example in can making. Imagine receiving two truckloads of equal weight, one segregated by alloy and one mixed. Their contribution to the next production run could differ substantially. Novelis reported 61 percent recycled content for FY26. That measures its material mix. A collection rate would need the amount of recoverable scrap generated as its denominator.

## Slide 10 · Strategic fit through postponement of finishing

*Thanvir Diouf Shafeeq · 9:25–10:30*

Suppose two extrusion profiles can use the same approved billet. Keeping the billet before the customer chooses a profile lets us pool some demand uncertainty. Once we use a particular die, the metal has a much narrower set of uses. That is the attraction of postponement. But imagine that the customer expects delivery in two days and the press has a four-day queue. Common stock would be physically present and still unable to meet that order. Our proposed test is to compare the remaining conversion lead time with the response time the customer accepts. Lavanya will now show how inventory, facilities and the other drivers enter that decision.

## Slide 11 · Logistical and cross-functional supply chain drivers

*Lavanya Joshi · 10:30–11:36*

Take that two-day order and assume a coil has just been delayed. The facilities question is which other line is qualified. The inventory question is whether it has unreserved stock of the correct grade. Transportation determines if an alternative shipment can reach the customer. Information determines how early we learn about the delay. Sourcing gives us the option of compatible external material, and pricing affects the cost and commercial terms of the response. These are the three logistical and three cross-functional drivers in our course framework. A change in one can shift cost to another. For example, a saving in freight can disappear if a larger shipment forces us to carry extra inventory.

## Slide 12 · Facilities, bottlenecks and qualified throughput

*Lavanya Joshi · 11:36–12:37*

Consider three different bottlenecks. At Renukoot, locating refining and smelting together shortens a transfer, while electricity remains essential to the process. At Dahej, the jetty, feed blend, processing capacity and acid dispatch must support the same production plan. At a rolling mill, the relevant line has to meet the customer’s product specification. In each case, we would first express the candidate constraints in a common unit, such as good tonnes of the promised grade per week. Then we would identify the lowest feasible rate. If finishing is the limiting stage, adding more intermediate metal can increase work in process while leaving customer throughput unchanged.

## Slide 13 · Little’s Law links transport lead time and pipeline inventory

*Lavanya Joshi · 12:37–13:39*

Follow a load through dispatch, transit and receipt. Define the end of the measurement as the time the material becomes available at the destination. In our illustrative lane, throughput averages one hundred tonnes per day and the average flow time is four days. Little’s Law gives four hundred tonnes in the pipeline. If average dwell increases by one day at the same throughput, a further hundred tonnes are tied up. That changes the comparison between carriers. A cheaper rate may require longer waits or larger loads. We would therefore compare the freight saving with the extra holding cost and the effect of arrival variability on delivery service.

## Slide 14 · Safety stock and the reorder point during lead time

*Lavanya Joshi · 13:39–14:42*

Let’s calculate a reorder point for a hypothetical, interchangeable grade. Assume average demand of one hundred tonnes per day, a daily standard deviation of twenty tonnes, and a fixed five-day lead time. Mean demand during lead time is five hundred tonnes. If daily demands are independent, their variances add, giving a standard deviation of twenty times the square root of five, about forty-five tonnes. Under a normal approximation, a 95 percent cycle service level uses a factor of about 1.645. That gives roughly seventy-four tonnes of safety stock and a reorder point of 574 tonnes. The trigger is inventory position, which includes open replenishment orders and subtracts backorders.

## Slide 15 · Continuous and periodic review use different protection periods

*Lavanya Joshi · 14:42–15:45*

Keep the same grade and service target. Under continuous review, we monitor inventory position and place a fixed lot when it reaches the reorder point. We protect five days of demand while waiting for the replenishment. Now suppose the buyer can place orders only every seven days. An order issued today must protect demand until the next review and through that next order’s five-day lead time. The protection period is twelve days. Applying the same demand assumptions gives an order-up-to level of about 1,314 tonnes. At review we order the positive gap between that level and inventory position. Only about 114 tonnes of the target are safety stock.

## Slide 16 · Why a 99% product fill rate can mean 50% OTIF

*Lavanya Joshi · 15:45–16:54*

Imagine two orders for the same grade, both due now. The first customer requests ninety units and receives all ninety on time. The second requests ten units and receives nine on time. We supplied ninety-nine of one hundred units immediately, giving a 99 percent product fill rate. But only one of the two orders arrived complete by its due date. Order fill rate and OTIF are both 50 percent in this particular example. They can differ when a complete order arrives late or when the agreed delivery date is later than the order date. To measure cycle service level we would also need to know which replenishment cycles experienced a stockout. Counting orders alone cannot tell us that.

## Slide 17 · Economic order quantity balances ordering and holding costs

*Lavanya Joshi · 16:54–17:58*

A replenishment decision has two parts. The reorder point determines when to act, and economic order quantity gives a starting point for how much to order. In our example, annual demand is 36,500 tonnes, placing an order costs ten thousand rupees, and holding a tonne for a year costs two thousand rupees. The EOQ formula gives about 604 tonnes. A lot of that size produces about 302 tonnes of average cycle inventory and lasts just over six days at average demand. A real metal shipment can face a rail minimum, vessel window or production campaign constraint. We would compare the nearest feasible lots on total cost before selecting one.

## Slide 18 · Inventory aggregation depends on grade compatibility

*Lavanya Joshi · 17:58–18:59*

Consider four locations with equal demand variability, independent demand, identical lead times and one interchangeable grade. If we completely pool their demand, the square root law gives a central safety stock equal to half their combined local safety stock at the same cycle service level. The saving shrinks if all sites experience demand peaks together. Physical aggregation also changes distance to the customer. Virtual aggregation keeps stock at several locations while sharing availability information, but a transfer still takes time. A practical middle ground is tailored aggregation. Pool slower common items and keep time-critical, uniquely approved stock close to where the customer needs it.

## Slide 19 · Sourcing strategy for bauxite, copper feed and returned alloy

*Lavanya Joshi · 18:59–20:00*

A captive mine, a concentrate contract and a scrap return agreement solve different sourcing problems. Captive aluminium feed gives the company visibility over upstream stages, though power and transport remain dependencies. For copper, compare two offers with the same price per delivered tonne of concentrate. The offer with less recoverable copper or higher impurity penalties may be more expensive per tonne of usable output. At Novelis, a tonne of returned scrap has to match a usable alloy stream and may need sorting or further processing. A common purchasing scorecard should therefore include usable yield, delivery timing, concentration and total landed cost alongside the purchase price.

## Slide 20 · Pricing, forward buying and demand signals

*Lavanya Joshi · 20:00–21:00*

Pricing can change the demand signal that operations sees. Novelis discloses a metal-price lag because purchases and customer sales can reflect prices from different periods. In India, Hindalco announced digital competitive tenders for spot sales of metallurgical-grade alumina. Here Utkal is the seller. Now consider a separate course example. A customer expecting a temporary discount to expire may bring an order forward even if its factory consumption is steady. If we extrapolate those orders, we can overproduce just before the customer enters a quiet buying period. Dhruv will pick up this distinction between orders and actual consumption when he discusses planning and the bullwhip effect.

## Slide 21 · Aggregate planning connects customer demand to dispatch

*Dhruv Agrawal · 21:00–22:01*

Start with a sales forecast expressed in finished tonnes. Planning translates it into the relevant grades and processing hours, checks raw material and utilities, and identifies any shortfall on the qualified line. If two customers need the same scarce finishing slot, someone must decide the allocation before sales can commit dates. Transport events then give dispatch and sales an opportunity to revise a promise in time. Hindalco reports logistics planning linked to sales and operations planning, along with ERP and transport visibility. The four steps on this slide show how we would use those capabilities in an aggregate plan. The sequence is our proposal for the case.

## Slide 22 · Information and sourcing connect suppliers, plants and customers

*Dhruv Agrawal · 22:01–23:04*

Coordination is easier to understand if we name the decision being coordinated. On the supplier side, copper due diligence assigns screening and risk responsibilities, while supplier finance affects payment timing. Inside the company, enterprise resource planning and warehouse visibility can show what is available, while transport systems show what is actually moving. At the customer boundary, Mahindra’s enclosure programme links an engineering change to the product the customer will accept. The Novelis scrap loop adds a return decision about material quality. An information system earns its cost when someone can act on an exception. A useful test is to trace a delayed receipt from first warning through the revised customer commitment.

## Slide 23 · Testing for a bullwhip effect in replenishment orders

*Dhruv Agrawal · 23:04–24:07*

Suppose a customer consumes a steady quantity each week but sends one large replenishment order every month. A supplier forecasting solely from the order file will see peaks the customer’s factory never experienced. To test for a bullwhip effect, match upstream orders to downstream demand for the same grade, unit and time interval. Compare their variances and allow for batching, stock movements and exceptional events. A ratio above one indicates amplification over that interval. We would then investigate the cause. A transport lot constraint suggests a scheduling response. A price-driven spike suggests reviewing the commercial calendar. Hindalco’s public data describe relevant mechanisms, but they do not establish a measured bullwhip ratio.

## Slide 24 · Copper sourcing economics under supplier uncertainty

*Dhruv Agrawal · 24:07–25:12*

Copper provides a useful check on the assumption that revenue growth means better supply chain performance. Between FY25 and FY26, Indian copper metal sales fell from 491 to 487 thousand tonnes. Revenue increased by about 27.7 percent, while EBITDA declined by about 7.1 percent. Management described a tight concentrate market and weak treatment and refining charges. These charges are part of the remuneration that a smelter earns for converting concentrate into refined metal. For a sourcing decision, we would separate metal prices, saleable volume, feed terms, by-product contributions and processing costs. The reported changes justify asking those questions. They do not quantify how much of the earnings movement came from any single factor.

## Slide 25 · Seasonal inventory and supply uncertainty in aluminium smelting

*Dhruv Agrawal · 25:12–26:19*

Now follow an energy input into an aluminium smelter. Coal arrives, the fuel stock supplies captive power generation, and electricity supports metal output. Reuters reported in November 2026 that quarterly coal cost had risen about five percent amid monsoon supply pressure. Consider the decision before the next monsoon. If historical receipts show a predictable seasonal shortfall, planners could build some fuel inventory early. Safety stock would cover the variability left after that expected shortfall is planned for. The trade-off is the cost of extra fuel storage and financing against the cost and service risk of insufficient power. We would need site-level receipts, daily burn and storage capacity to set a target. No site target is assumed here.

## Slide 26 · Capacity strategy for battery foil at Aditya

*Dhruv Agrawal · 26:19–27:22*

The same aluminium chain looks different when we start at the customer end. Suppose a customer needs a qualified batch of battery foil by a specified date. First reserve the foil-finishing time and allow for inspection and yield. Next calculate how much compatible rolled feedstock must be ready when that slot begins. Then work back to the primary metal requirement. In January 2026, Hindalco announced commissioning of flat-rolled product and battery-foil facilities in Odisha, including an addition of 170,000 tonnes per year of flat-rolled capacity. That rate belongs to the feedstock stage. To make a delivery promise we still need the qualified foil line’s available hours, saleable yield and release date.

## Slide 27 · SWOT of sourcing, energy efficiency and product development

*Dhruv Agrawal · 27:22–28:27*

Our strength is an established material connection, either a captive aluminium route or a customer scrap loop. Such links can improve visibility over inputs. Our weakness is the power intensity of aluminium smelting. Even with captive bauxite, the process carries exposure to fuel cost through electricity. The opportunity is customer demand for engineered EV battery enclosures. Chakan’s ten thousand deliveries to Mahindra show that this product has an actual industrial customer. Additional vehicle programmes would require their own designs, qualifications and orders. For the threat, tight copper concentrate markets can put pressure on processing terms. Each item leads to a different test: usable feed, energy cost per tonne, qualified demand or conversion contribution.

## Slide 28 · Comparing inventory policies on OTIF and average inventory

*Dhruv Agrawal · 28:27–29:28*

How would we test our proposals without committing to a new company-wide inventory policy? Select a common grade and a customer-qualified family. Rebuild the orders as they appeared when each promise was made, including requested dates, original commitments, receipts, releases and partial deliveries. Then replay a continuous review policy and a periodic review policy using only information available on each historical date. Compare original-date OTIF, shortage units, average inventory and expedite costs. If the highest-service policy simply triples stock, management can see what it is paying for. If a policy reduces inventory while preserving service, the result is a reason to try a limited operating pilot.

## Slide 29 · Sourcing and capacity strategy before expansion

*Dhruv Agrawal · 29:28–30:28*

Before adding capacity, we would apply one test to each system. For Indian copper, calculate the tonnes of compatible feed that can reach Dahej in each delivery window, including suppliers that share a gateway. For Indian aluminium, check whether planned rolled feedstock, finishing hours and inspection releases can support the battery-foil orders in the same month. For Novelis, measure usable recovered alloy against the full cost of return freight, segregation and remelting. These tests can reveal whether the next investment belongs in equipment, a supply agreement, customer qualification or material handling. The scorecard should pair good throughput with OTIF, total landed cost and working capital.

## Slide 30 · Strategic fit at the customer’s promised date

*Dhruv Agrawal · 30:28–31:30*

Return to the question we opened with. What does it take to promise a tonne next week? In Indian aluminium, secure the continuous upstream flow, then reserve the finishing route that can make the customer’s exact product. In copper, secure compatible feed and ensure that the processing and co-product outlets can support the plan. At Novelis, match the customer’s qualification to regional rolling capacity and recover a usable alloy through the return loop. Across the three businesses, our proposed measure is the quantity the customer can actually use by the promised date, together with the cost and inventory required to deliver it. Thank you. We welcome your questions.

## Slide 31 · Appendix A · Reorder point and lead-time demand

*Q&A · Q&A only*

With independent daily demand, a five-day lead time multiplies the variance by five and the standard deviation by the square root of five. That gives 44.72 tonnes. A one-sided 95 percent cycle service target gives 73.57 tonnes of safety stock. Add that to 500 tonnes of mean lead-time demand to obtain a reorder point of 573.57 tonnes. A variable lead time would need an additional variance term or a simulation using actual paired histories.

## Slide 32 · Appendix B · Expected shortage, fill rate and average inventory

*Q&A · Q&A only*

At the 95 percent cycle service target, the normal loss function gives an expected shortage of approximately 0.93 tonnes per replenishment cycle. With a 604-tonne lot and backorders, the estimated product fill rate is one minus 0.93 divided by 604, or about 99.85 percent. Average on-hand inventory is approximately half the lot plus safety stock, about 376 tonnes. A stockout in five percent of cycles is compatible with this unit fill rate because each shortage is small relative to the replenishment lot.

## Slide 33 · Appendix C · Copper capacity and throughput measures

*Q&A · Q&A only*

Cathode capacity and continuous cast rod capacity describe successive processes. A cathode can be sold or converted into rod, so adding their annual capacities would double-count some material. Likewise, FY26 metal sales of 487 thousand tonnes already included 373 thousand tonnes of rod sales. Rod represented about 76.6 percent of metal sales. Before estimating utilisation we would reconcile production, opening and closing stock, external purchases, and the period and grade covered by the capacity denominator.
